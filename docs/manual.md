# OCAD Runner user manual

How to do the common tasks in OCAD Runner, and what each of its major windows holds. Terms in capitals (Terrain Area, Course, Assisted Run) are explained in the [glossary](glossary.md).

Keys and mouse buttons marked **(configurable)** are defaults that each Profile can change in its Profile options, under Keys and mouse. All other input is fixed. The full list of a Run's keys and mouse actions is in [controls.md](controls.md).

## Getting started

OCAD Runner needs a screen of at least 1280 × 800 pixels, not counting the system's panels. On a smaller screen it says so and exits.

The game always starts in a window, on the Profile screen. A Profile with **Full screen** on makes the game full screen once it is chosen.

The way from an OCAD map to a Run:

1. Make a **Terrain Area** from the OCAD map in the Terrain Area Editor.
2. Make an **Event** with a Course on that Terrain Area in the Event Editor.
3. **Run** the Course from the main menu's **Run** window.
4. Look at the **analysis**, which opens after the Run, or later from **Analyse**.

## Tasks

### Make or choose a Profile

1. On the Profile screen ("Who is playing?"), write a name under **New Profile** and press **Create**.
2. Choose the Profile's **Language** and press **OK**. The new Profile is made and chosen, and the game goes on to the main menu in that language. The Profile options can change the language later. **Cancel** makes no Profile.
3. To play an existing Profile, select it in the list and press **Select**.

To change Profile later, press **Change Profile** in the main menu. To rename one, select it and press **Rename…**: its Runs, Terrain Areas and Events stay its own.

### Set the Profile options

1. In the main menu, press **Options…** (beside Change Profile).
2. Change what you want on the tabs: **Game**, **Keyboard**, **Mouse**, **Compass**, **Analyse**, **Edit** or **Sound**. On **Edit**: **Warn about places near the map edge** (on at first), the Event Editor's outline and warning for Controls, Starts and Finishes too close to the edge of the drawn map; **Show control codes when editing a Course** (on at first), the Event Editor's map labels the Controls with their codes in place of 1, 2, 3, …, and only **Preview Course** shows the numbers; **Default difficulty** (Orange at first, Green to Black), the difficulty of every Course the Event Editor makes for you (a new Event's first Course, **New Course** and imported Courses) and the Difficulty **Add Controls…** opens on; **Other Controls' opacity** (35% at first, 5–100%), how strongly the Controls, Starts and Finishes outside the chosen Course show in the Event Editor, with a preview of one Control in a Course and two outside it; **Tree distance** (800 m at first, 50–1800 m), how far away trees are drawn in the 3D views of the editors and Analyse (a shorter one is faster in big forests; it takes effect the next time a 3D view is built); and **Diagnostic Tools** (off at first), which shows Copy code and Paste code in the Terrain Area editor and turns on its map keys P, L and Backspace (go to, copy and clear a place) and adds a **Diagnostics** submenu to its map's right-click menu: **Copy position to clipboard** (L, for the place right-clicked) and **Enter coordinates…** (P). On **Analyse**: **Course opacity**, how strongly the Course shows on Analyse's map; **Inactive Legs' opacity**, how strongly the other Legs show while a Leg is selected; **Selected Run's opacity**, how strongly the selected Run's track (the one with the marks) shows, and **Other Runs' opacity**, the other Runs' tracks shown with it, both 70% at first; **Replay at actual speed** (off at first), which replays a Run as fast as it was played; and **Open on**, the tab Analyse opens on, Map (at first) or Numbers. Analyse's other choices are not here: Analyse keeps them itself, as you left them. On **Sound**: **Silent Mode** (off at first) turns every sound off; below it **Start Countdown**, the start clock's beeps, and **Punching**, the beeps at the Controls and the Finish (both on at first), greyed while Silent Mode is on.
3. Press **OK** to keep the changes, or **Cancel** to drop them. **All defaults** sets every tab back to its defaults.

### Change a key

1. In the Profile options, on **Keyboard**, choose **Custom** under Keys. Only Custom's keys can be changed. To start from a ready-made setup, press **Copy keys from…** beside it and choose Default, Catching Features #1 or Catching Features #2: Custom gets its keys.
2. Click the slot beside the action, then press the new key. Esc cancels the wait.
3. Right-click a slot to clear it. **Default** sets that action's keys back.
4. If another action had the key, it is taken from that action, and a note says so.

### Make a Terrain Area from an OCAD map

1. In the main menu, press **Terrain Area Editor**, then **OCAD File…**, and choose the `.ocd` file. Until a file is open, the map area is white and says how to open one.
2. The **Symbol Links** window opens. Check the Mapping Standard, then go through the unresolved Symbols at the top of the list. Tick a guess to confirm it, or right-click the Link for other choices. You can also use **Confirm guesses** and **Skip unguessed** to handle them all at once. Press **Done**.
3. Draw the **Terrain Area Boundary**. **Polygon** draws a shape: click the corners and click the first corner to finish. **Rectangle** draws a rectangle from two opposite corners. The button of the active mode is in boldface and stays on until you click it again or press Esc; while it is on, a boundary that fits the mode stays and its corners can be dragged (a corner grows when the pointer is near it). In Polygon mode, dragging from a point on an edge adds a new corner there, and Delete over a corner removes it (a polygon keeps at least three). An area cannot be smaller than 100 m in any direction. **OCAD's export area** takes the export area set in OCAD.
4. Choose the **Terrain Style** and check the **Contour Interval**, then press **Build Terrain Area**.
5. If some contours' uphill side is unclear, the **Slope Direction editor** opens first. Fix the red lines (see below), then press **Build**.
6. Check the result in the layers or the **3D View**. Under the Build button, the editor says which contour ends were left open and where the ground drops or rises sharply over a long stretch, often a contour drawn on two levels. Both are marked on the **Contour Model** layer: open ends with rings, sharp drops and rises with a ring and a cross. Fix such contours in OCAD and open the file again, or draw the boundary round another part of the map.
7. Press **Save As…**, write a name (and the text about the map if you like), and save.

### Make a Terrain Area from a random map

A first test of the Map Generator: a made-up orienteering map, and its terrain, with no OCAD File.

1. In the Terrain Area Editor, press **Generate Random Map…**.
2. Choose the **Map Recipe** (what the map is made like), the **Size** in metres, the **Scale**, the **Contour Interval**, the **Steepness** from Flat to Steep, how much **Level ground** (at most 50 %, 30 % at first), and whether it has **Settlements** (on at first), **Water bodies** (off at first) and **Power lines** (off at first), how much **Dense vegetation** there is, how **Dry hilltops** are, how many **Footpaths**, how many **Stone walls** and how many **Open forest areas** (clear-cuts and felled areas) there are (sliders from 0 to 3, 1 as the Map Recipe has it; dry hilltops have rough open land and bare rock). The **Seed** picks the map: the same seed and settings always make the same map, and **New seed** draws another.
3. Press **Generate**. Making the map takes up to a minute.
4. The map opens with its boundary already set and its Symbols linked, so there is no Symbol Links window and no Slope Direction editor. The panel says what it was made with.
5. To try another map with the same settings, press **Generate new**, which draws another seed and makes the map again. For other settings, press **Generate Random Map…** again.
6. To keep or share exactly this map, press **Copy code**: the clipboard then holds the map's Settings Code, one string with the generator's exact version and every setting. **Paste code** makes the map again from a code on the clipboard (the settings shown are then those of the code). A code only works with the generator version that made it; a Terrain Area saved before codes existed has none until it is generated again.
7. Choose the **Terrain Style** and press **Build Terrain Area**, then **Save As…**, as for an OCAD map. The Terrain Area keeps its map, so it opens again with **Terrain Area…** without any file.

If **Generate Random Map…** is greyed out, the Map Generator is not installed with this game.

### Fix slope directions

1. In the Slope Direction editor, red lines have issues. The arrows point downhill.
2. Click a line to turn its direction. Shift+click accepts it as it is. Either way the line turns green.
3. To find the lines still red, press ◀ or ▶ under the count at the top (or Page Up / Page Down): they go to the previous or next red line you have not changed, round and round from the line you changed last, and show it alone, highlighted, in the middle of the map. They are greyed out when no red line is left. While one line is highlighted (this way or with Ctrl+click), F flips its direction, as a click on it does.
4. Press **Check again** to see whether your changes raised new issues.
5. Press **Build** once no red line is left.
6. If you changed lines, you are asked whether to save their directions for other cutouts of this map, counted in contour pieces (a contour joined across gaps is often several pieces in OCAD) (**Save**, **Don't save**, or **Cancel** to go back). Saved lines are dark violet the next time you build any part of the same map, and need no correcting; they can still be turned. A line changed in OCAD since is not taken: correct it again.

To review the directions later, press **Slopes…** beside the Build button.

To see or delete saved directions, press **Saved Slopes…** in the Slope Direction editor: it lists them by OCAD File, with how many contour pieces and when they were saved last. The bin (🗑) deletes a file's saved directions after a question; a piece saved from another file too stays saved for it. Lines shown dark violet keep their direction until you close the editor.

### Open, change or delete a saved Terrain Area

1. In the Terrain Area Editor, press **Terrain Area…**.
2. Select one, then press **Open** (or double-click it) or the bin (🗑, Delete…). Deleting moves the file to the system's trash.
3. After changes, press **Save…** to save over it, or **Save As…** for a new Terrain Area. Rebuilding a Terrain Area that has Events gives their Courses new Course Versions.

### Make a Terrain Style

1. In the Terrain Area Editor, press **Terrain Styles…**.
2. Select a style and press **New…**. The new style starts as a copy of the selected one.
3. Change values on the **Runnability** and **Roughness** tabs. Changes are saved at once.
4. Press **Close**. The selected style is now chosen for the next build.

### Make an Event and a Course

1. In the main menu, press **Event Editor**. Until an Event is open, the map area is white and says how to make or open one.
2. Press **New Event…**, select the Terrain Area, optionally write an **Event description** (free text about the Event) under the list, and press **New Event** (or double-click the Terrain Area). The new Event has one Course, A. The description can be changed when saving.
3. Under **Courses**, choose **All Controls**. Under **Map Tools**, choose **Add Start** (the triangle), **Add Control** (the circle) or **Add Finish** (the double circle), and click on the map to place it. Nothing can be placed or moved outside the Terrain Area Boundary: a box by where you clicked says so, until you click again, and a dragged Control, flag, Start or Finish goes back. A Control, Start or Finish placed where something else is wrong with it, such as a flag, Start or Finish in impassable ground, is placed, and a box by where you clicked says what, until you click again. A Control's flag, Start or Finish (from an Event made before, or an imported one) outside the Boundary or standing on a boulder, a cliff or impassable ground is an error: its row in the Course is red, the Course is listed in red, and the warnings below the Course say what. A Control, Start or Finish closer than 8 mm on the map to the edge of the drawn map, where the ground is less exact, also has an orange ring round its circle; a Terrain Area built before version 0.7.0 does not know its edge until it is built again in the Terrain Area Editor. One too close to another is not placed, and a box by where you clicked says why, until you click again: Controls' flags at least 30 m apart (15 m on sprint maps), a Start or Finish at least as far from every flag; a Start at least 100 m from every other Start, and a Finish from every other Finish. New Controls get codes from 31, never 66, 68, 69, 86, 89, 96, 98 or 99. An Event can have several of each. Right-click removes what is under the mouse, from the Event and every Course; a Control a Course visits only after asking (see below). Drag to move the map, and use the wheel or the + and − keys (keypad too) to zoom; the map always fills the view, as in Analyse. No button or list holds the keyboard in the editors, so the keys always reach the map; a click on the map leaves a text field.
4. To move a Control, Start or Finish, choose **Move** (the mouse arrow) and drag it: the flag goes along with its circle. A place too close to another goes back, and a box by where you let go says why until you click again. A click or drag anywhere inside a circle, triangle or the Finish's outer ring takes it. In a Course, Move also drags a Control's number to where you want it on the map; right-click a moved number to let it place itself again. Numbers not moved go by themselves at the side with most room, clear of the rest of the Course. To move a Control's flag away from its circle's centre, choose **Move Flag** (the flag with a small arrow): select the Control and click where the flag stands, or drag the flag. Only the flag of a point object (a boulder, a knoll, a pit…) or a line object (a cliff, an earth bank, a stone wall…) can stand apart from its circle; for areas it stays at the centre. In the Add modes a drag moves the map.
5. Check each Control's **Control Description**. It is guessed from the map, but only when the guess says where the Control is: a point feature, a line at a junction, crossing, bend or end, or an area with a side, edge, corner or part. Otherwise only the code is filled in. Cliffs and earth banks are never described by their end: above one, with **Top**; at the foot of a long one (30 m or more), with the foot and its direction; at a shorter one, the cliff alone or the earth bank with **Foot**. A cliff at its foot and a boulder get their height in column F, measured on the 3D terrain to the nearest 0.5 m (a boulder at least 1 m). A Control on a knoll gets **Top**. The Course's Controls are listed as a description sheet, as on paper but without the Course's name and length at the top (its length and climb are on the line under the sheet): the Start's row, a row for each Control and the Finish's. To change a column, click it in a row and choose its symbol (in column F, also a size); a click anywhere else, or on the same column of the row again, closes the choice without changing anything. Pointing at a column names its symbol. A click on column A or B selects the row, and again lets go of it. A description you change is **Locked**: moving the Control keeps it. The padlock beside each row shows whether it is locked (faint and open when not); click it to lock or unlock the row, or use **Locked** under the sheet for the selected row. Untick **Locked**, or press **Suggest from map** under the sheet, to have the selected row guessed again.
6. Under **Courses**, choose a Course, or press **New Course**. With **Add Control** chosen under **Map Tools**, click the Controls on the map in running order. Each goes in after the selected row of the Course, or before the Finish when no row is selected. A click on empty ground places a new Control into the Course. A Control may come more than once, never twice in a row.
7. With **Start** or **Finish** chosen, click one of the Event's Starts or Finishes to make it the Course's, or on empty ground to place a new one for it.
8. Change the order with ▲ and ▼ beside a row of the sheet, and take a Control off the Course with ✕, or by right-clicking it on the map.
9. Where a Course's line or circle covers important map detail, choose **Cut** under **Map Tools** (the circle and line with scissors) and drag along the line or round the circle (or either of the Finish's rings, which are cut together): the part dragged over is cut out, on every map of this Course. Cuts that overlap join. Right-click a cut to close it. A click still selects, and a drag elsewhere moves the map. Cuts on Legs or circles the Course no longer has are dropped when the Event is saved. On a **Corridor** or **Line** Course (its Type is set in Course Settings, step 10), the Legs follow its course line, straight at first: with **Move**, drag a Leg's line to bend it there, drag a bend (a small white square) to move it, and right-click a bend to remove it; each drag can be undone. Moving a Control moves the line's end, its bends staying. To make a Leg straight again, select the row of the place it starts from and press **Straighten Leg** under the rows. Other Types draw their Legs straight, keeping the bends for when the Course is made Corridor or Line again. On a **Line** Course, **Snap to Lines** (the magnet after the Map Tools, on at first and remembered by your Profile) makes a bend you drag snap to a road, path, stream or stone wall within 12 pixels of it, or to the middle of a paved area narrow enough to be a road (at most 20 m wide; not under a building or wall drawn on it, nor where another area is drawn over it), a path ending at its edge joining it, a junction, crossing or end before any other point; between two snapped bends the line follows it, round its bends and across junctions, the shortest way along them, unless that is more than twice as long as the straight line. Hold **Alt** while dragging to do the other: place the bend freely with Snap to Lines on, or snap it with Snap to Lines off. Snapped bends are white circles instead of squares, and a stretch that follows is drawn on a light cyan band, only while editing: the runner's map, Preview Course and Analyse show the plain line. Dragging a followed stretch adds a bend on it; removing a bend between two snapped ones makes the line follow from one to the other. A Terrain Area built before Snap to Lines has the magnet greyed out until it is built again; lines already saved stay as they are, also when the Terrain Area is built again, until you drag their bends.
10. Press **Course Settings…** to rename the Course, set its **Difficulty**, **Course setter** and **Course description**, tick **Guided Punching**, choose the **Allowed Aids**, the **Flag Size** or the **Map Type**, or untick **Show Full Course** to highlight the next legs, and press **OK**. To set them for every Course at once, choose **All Controls** and press **Settings for all Courses…**: only what you change is set, in every Course.
11. Press **Save As…**, write the Event's name, and save. Every Course needs a Start, at least one Control and a Finish, and a name of its own. Saving computes the Perfect Race.

To make a **Score** Course, choose **Score** as its Type in **Course Settings…** and set its time limit. Add its Controls as any Course's; each comes only once. Its rows are listed by code, each showing the code alone with no number, as the Controls have no order, and the line under the sheet shows its time limit in place of the length: no ▲ ▼ but a box for each Control's points (10 at first); **Points by code tens** under the rows makes each worth the tens of its code (31 to 39 give 3). Undo covers the points. The map shows the Controls' circles with their codes and points, as 31 (10), and no lines. Saving finds the routes between every two of its places, which takes a while the first time (about 7 s for 12 Controls, 20 s for 20 on a 1 km map); later saves reuse them for the places that did not move. The Perfect Race is the order of Controls scoring the most points within the time limit, the fastest such one, and the panel gives its points, Controls, time, length and climb. A Control no route reaches from the Start, or that has none to the Finish, stops the Event from being saved.

To delete a Control, Start or Finish from the Event, choose **Delete** under **Map Tools** (the circle with a ✕) and click it, under All Controls or in a Course. One no Course uses goes at once. Otherwise a question names the Courses first (five at most, then "(and more)"), and **Yes** deletes it, **No** keeps it: a Control is taken off the Courses visiting it; Courses starting or finishing at a deleted Start or Finish move to the first one left, which the question names, or have none. Right-clicking under **All Controls** asks the same. **Undo** brings it back.

**Duplicate** copies the chosen Course under a new name, the bin (🗑) deletes it (its Runs are kept), and ▲ and ▼ next to **New Course** change the order the main menu lists the Courses in.

To see the flags where the runner will meet them, press **3D View** at the bottom left of the map. The Course is drawn on the ground. Click a Control's row, or a Control on the 2D map before switching, and the camera goes to its flag, so you can check that it stands where the description says. The Map Tools work only in the **2D View**.

To see the map as the runner will, tick **Preview Course** under **Course Settings…** (off at first, and again for each Event made or opened). The 2D map then shows the chosen Course as its Run's map does at the Start, for its Orienteering Type: its Map Type and Black overprint; only the windows on Window, the band on Corridor (without the Legs' lines unless Show the course line is ticked), the line on Line (its rings as its Rings say), the circles with their points on Score, the north lines and border on Compass only, the map mirrored without its text on Mirrored, the first legs highlighted with Highlight; no flags, no Terrain Area Boundary and no Perfect Race. The Map Tools work on it as usual: place, move, cut, bend and delete on the map the runner sees, on a Mirrored Course on the mirrored map; the selected place's ring, a cut being drawn and a Corridor or Line Course's bends show over it, and the windows and band follow once you let go. It needs a Course with a Start, Controls and a Finish.

### Import Courses from a course-setting program

Courses set in OCAD or Purple Pen can be brought into an Event, when they were set on the map the Terrain Area was made from.

1. In Purple Pen, export the Courses as IOF XML (version 3.0 or 2.0.3). From OCAD, the course-setting project's OCAD File itself can be imported, or its IOF XML export. Of a relay or leg variation, the first branch is imported.
2. In the Event Editor, make or open the Event, press **Import Courses…** under the Courses, and choose the file.
3. A window lists the file's Courses, each with its number of Controls and its length. Tick the Courses to import (**All** ticks every one, **None** clears them), and press **Import**. Nothing is ticked at first, and Import stays off until a Course is. Beside each Course is a drop-down for its **Difficulty**, set to your Profile's default difficulty; change it for the Courses that differ.
4. A window says which Courses were imported, which were left out and why, which Controls got new codes, and the warnings. A Course is left out when it does not lie wholly within the Terrain Area, when one of its Controls, Starts or Finishes is too close to another (the same rules as placing them: see above), when it lacks a Start or a Finish (as OCAD's routes for putting out the Controls do), or when the Event already has it. A Control with a code the game never uses (66, 68, 69, 86, 89, 96, 98 or 99), or a code another Control of the Event has, gets a new code. A Start or Finish standing on a boulder, a cliff or impassable ground is warned about; how near the edge a Control stands is not. A file whose Controls all lie outside the Terrain Area is refused.
5. Check the imported Controls' descriptions and flags, and save. An OCAD File's descriptions come with its Controls; IOF XML has none, so those are guessed from the map once, as Add Controls describes its places: junctions, crossings and bends, spurs and re-entrants on the contours, and column C where features alike lie in the circle. Either way **Locked** is ticked, so moving a Control keeps its description. Circles and lines cut in an OCAD File (OCAD 2018 or later) are cut the same way in the game; IOF XML holds no cuts. A flag that would stand on a boulder, a cliff or impassable ground is not moved: the window says which Controls' flags do, and you move them yourself.

A Control, Start or Finish within 5 m of one of the Event's, a Control with the same code, is taken as that one, so importing the same file again adds nothing new. In a new Event, the imported Courses take the place of the empty Course A. **Undo** takes the whole import away again, its Courses too, and **Redo** brings it back.

### Add Controls by Difficulty, and a random Course

The game can put Controls in the forest for you, on features that suit a Difficulty.

1. In the Event Editor, make or open the Event, and press **Add Controls…** beside Import Courses….
2. Choose the **Difficulty** (your Profile's Default difficulty at first, set on the Profile options' Edit tab) and how many **Controls**, up to 50 at a time, and press **Add**.
3. A window says which Controls were added. When too few places suit the Difficulty, fewer are added than asked for.
4. To have a Course made as well, tick **Make a Course** before pressing Add. Give it a **Name**, a **Characteristic** (below), an **Ideal length** and an **Ideal number of Controls** (both filled in for the Difficulty when you choose it), the **Most line crossings** it may have (0 to 4), the **Smallest Control angle** between the Leg coming in and the Leg going out at a Control, and the **Leg length limit** (50 to 2000 m, 600 m at first): no Leg of the Course is longer; it is a limit, not a length the longest Leg must have. The number of Controls to add can then be 0, to use only those the Event has. When the options can never make a Course, the dialog says why and Add is off.
5. The **Characteristic** says what the Course is made to be like: **None** (only the length, the number of Controls and the rules; no Control visited twice), **Direction changes**, **Varied leg length**, **Homogenous leg length**, **Butterfly** or **Course crossing**; each entry's tooltip says what it means. It starts on None, then on the one you used last. Direction changes, Homogenous and Varied leg length come as near as the Controls allow, and the window after Add says what came of it: how many pairs of Controls in a row turn to opposite sides (a turn counts at 30° or more from straight on), or the Legs' shortest, longest and average. A **Butterfly** has one Control in the middle visited three times, with a loop of two Controls or more on each side of it, and needs at least 9 Controls; at its centre any turn is allowed but going straight back. The window says which Control is the centre. **Course crossing** makes the Course's lines cross each other at least as often as the field, now **Fewest line crossings** (1 to 4), says; only clear crossings count, the Legs crossing at 30° or more and at least a circle's width from every circle; the window says how many it got.

The Course is made of all the Event's Controls that suit the Difficulty, those on other Courses too, and the Event's Start and Finish, so the Event must have both first: without them **Make a Course** is greyed, and a line in red under it says which is missing and to place it with **Add Start** or **Add Finish** under Map Tools. It comes as near the ideal length and number of Controls as those Controls allow, and never passes through another of its Controls' circles. The window then says how long it came out, with how many Controls, and how often its lines cross, and the new Course is chosen. **Undo** takes it away with the Controls added. When no Course can be made but Controls were added, the window says why and asks whether to add the Controls anyway, without a Course: **Yes** keeps them, **No** takes them away again.

Green puts them on paths, at junctions, bends and ends, and where a path crosses a stream, ditch or stone wall (a wall that ends at a path and goes on at its other side crosses it); White on line features such as paths, streams, ditches, walls and fences; Yellow on them or on a clear feature right beside one; Orange on clear features, such as a ruin, a large boulder, a cliff or a pond, near a line feature; Violet on clear features anywhere; Blue and Black on any feature, small ones too, away from the paths when they can, and most often on spurs and re-entrants, on the distinct bends of the map's contours (on form lines only now and then) (a Terrain Area built before version 0.11.0 has to be built again for them). A cliff is used at its foot, with its height, never on top; a point feature's circle, such as a boulder's, is on it and the flag beside it, on the side the description says (a knoll's at its foot); a small ruin drawn as a point mostly has its Control in the middle of it, the description naming no side, and now and then at one of its corners, the circle there too, described as its outer or inner corner). From Orange up, none is on a road or path, and none is ever on a less distinct small path. Where more features like the Control's lie in its circle, such as two boulders or two path junctions, column C says which (the northern, the south-western, the middle, …); Green and White use such places only when no other is left (a Terrain Area built before version 0.11.0 has to be built again for the small paths and the wall crossings). They keep 60 m (30 m on a sprint map) from every other Control, Start and Finish, and 100 m from the edge of the Terrain Area. The new Controls are in no Course: add them to Courses as any other. Areas, such as clearings and buildings drawn as areas, are not used for now, nor single dots of stony ground, boulder fields or broken ground. Their descriptions are guessed from the feature, a path branching off another described as a junction, and are not Locked. **Undo** takes them all away again.

### Open, change or delete a saved Event

1. In the Event Editor, press **Open…**.
2. Select one, then press **Open** (or double-click it) or the bin (🗑, Delete…). Deleting moves the file to the system's trash; its Runs can still be analysed.
3. After changes, press **Save…** to save over it, or **Save As…** for a new Event under another name. The Save window says when a Course with Runs gets a new Course Version.

Leaving an Event with changes not saved (New Event…, Open…, the house (⌂, Back to menu) or closing the window) asks first whether to discard them.

**Undo** (↶, Ctrl+Z) and **Redo** (↷, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z), at the top left of the right panel, step through the changes to the Controls, Starts and Finishes and to the Courses' rows, cuts and numbers since the Event was made or opened; a whole drag is one step. Managing the Courses (New Course, Duplicate, Delete, ▲ ▼) and the Course Settings are not undone.

A click on a Control, Start or Finish selects it, to see or edit its description, whichever Map Tool is chosen; in a Course, with **Control** chosen, a click on a Control adds it to the Course instead.

### Export Courses as IOF XML

1. In the Event Editor, right-click the 2D map where the right-click does nothing else (as for Export to PDF).
2. Under **Export Course**, choose **IOF XML 3.0** or **IOF XML 2.0.3**. Both are greyed, with the reason in the tooltip, in the Terrain Area Editor and in an Event without a complete Course.
3. Choose where to save the file and its name; a dialog tells how many Courses were written.

Every complete Course of the Event is written (Leg Practice's are not), with its Start, Controls and Finish at their map positions in mm on the paper at the map's scale, so a course-setting program such as OCAD or Purple Pen, or Import Courses here, places them on the same map. Control Descriptions, cuts, course lines and the Orienteering Types' settings are not part of the files.

### Export a map to PDF

1. In the Terrain Area Editor or the Event Editor, right-click the 2D map where the right-click does nothing else: not on a Control, Start or Finish, a bend or a cut in the Event Editor, and not while drawing the Terrain Area Boundary in the Terrain Area Editor.
2. Under **Export to PDF**, choose **Map only**, **Map with Course** (the Event Editor, for a Course chosen with a Start, Controls and a Finish), **Map with hillshade** or **Runnability map** (in the Terrain Area Editor after a build), or **Slope Direction** (the Terrain Area Editor, after a build: the contours with arrows pointing downhill). A greyed choice says why in its tooltip.
3. Choose where to save the PDF and its name.

The PDF holds the whole map of the Terrain Area (in the Terrain Area Editor the area inside the Terrain Area Boundary, or the whole OCAD map without one) at the map's own scale, on the smallest of A4, A3 and A2, portrait or landscape, that holds it. A map larger than A2 is cut to its middle part, and a dialog and the line under the map say so. **Map with Course** shows the Course as the runner's map has it (its Map Type, Window, Corridor, Compass only, Mirrored and black overprint), but always the whole Course, with its Control Descriptions where they cover no part of it, the page made larger when they do not fit otherwise.

### Run a Course

1. In the main menu, press **Run**. In the Run screen, choose an Event, then a Course. Each Course is a row: its name and number of Controls, then its Orienteering Type (Normal, Score 30 min, …), its difficulty as a colour (its name in the tooltip), a star for the first Run of it or a circling arrow for a rerun (you have run this Course Version before; Leg Practice does not count), and a sun for daylight or a crescent for night orienteering. A Course's tooltip says how its Type is run, names its Map Type when it is not the normal map, whether the map highlights the next legs, and the aids it does not allow, such as the compass.
2. Optionally choose a **Game speed** (1x to 4x; the Profile options set the one offered first, 2x for a new Profile).
3. Press **Start**, or double-click the Course. After the five-second Start Countdown the clock starts.
4. Find the Controls in order. A Punch happens on its own when you come within 1.2 m of a flag (on a Course with **No flag**, within 5 m of where the flag would stand). It beeps, and **Punched** with the Control's code shows at the top of the screen, where the Start Countdown was, for two seconds before it fades away.
5. The Run ends at the Finish, and the analysis opens.

On a **Score** Course, punch any of its Controls, in any order, within its time limit: each is worth the points printed by its code on the map, as 31 (10), and a Control punched is drawn toned down. Within 50 m of one of the Course's Controls, its Control Description row shows (the nearest one's). The time left shows at the top of the screen, and once over the limit how far over and what it costs: the points set for each minute begun. Go to the Finish when you choose; it ends the Run even with Controls left. A Finish at the Start punches only once you have been 10 m away from it.

On a **Window** Course, the map shows only the ground round the Start, each Control and the Finish, within the Course's radius (the Course list shows it, as **Course 2 · Window 75 m**); elsewhere it is blank paper with the map's north lines drawn whole across it, as on Compass only, and the Course's lines drawn over it. Show Position shows the same windows.

On a **Corridor** Course, the map shows only a band along the course line, as wide as the Course sets (**Course 2 · Corridor 80 m**); elsewhere it is blank paper with the map's north lines drawn whole across it, as on Window, and the Start, Controls and Finish drawn over it; the lines between them only when the Course has **Show the course line** ticked, so the band alone leads the way. Leave the band where you like: there is no penalty.

On a **Line** Course, the map shows only the line, from the Start to the Finish; its Controls are on it, but their rings show only if the Course says so (once found, or all the time). Follow the line and punch the Controls in order as you find them. Pass one by and punch a later one, or the Finish, and it is missed: each missed Control adds the Course's cost to your time (5 min at first). A Control that is not on the Course gives no Punch. After the Finish, Analyse shows every ring.

On a **Memory** Course, read the map and remember it. With **At Controls only**, the map can be read only within the Course's distance (15 m at first) of the last Control you punched, or of the Start before the first; elsewhere the map is blank paper saying **Map only at a Control** (the compass still works). With **Map time per leg**, each Leg allows the Course's time of map reading (10 s at first), shown at the top of the screen as **Map 7 s** and counted afresh on each Leg; reading it longer costs time for each second more begun (5 s at first), added to your total and shown at the top as it grows. A blank map counts no time. Show Position shows the map as usual (it is Assistance).

On a **Place the flags** Course, the Controls have no flags. Go to them in order with the map and the Control Descriptions as usual, and where you judge the flag should stand, press **P** (Place flag) to place it: "Control 1 is placed" shows, the flag stands on the ground where you placed it, and the next Control's description follows. Each flag is placed once, and the next only after you have been 10 m from the last. With **Ask before placing** on the Course, P first asks "Place Control 1 here?", the clock stopped and the map and terrain hidden: **Yes** (Enter, or P again) places it, **No** (Esc) does not. A flag within the Course's free distance (5 m at first) of its true place costs nothing, each metre beyond it begun costs time (2 s at first), and one more than the far distance off (100 m at first) a flat time (5 min at first), all added to your total. You learn how far off each was after the Finish. Reaching the Finish with a flag not placed is a Mispunch.

To pause, press Esc for the Run menu. From there, **Abandon (DNF)** ends the Run as Did Not Finish.

### How fast you run

Everyone runs under the same Speed Model, so Runs can be compared. On flat, open ground (Runnability 1) you can run at 3:30 min/km. Forest and other slow ground slow you by its Runnability. Slopes slow you too, by the same share on any ground:

| Slope | Pace | Slope | Pace |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uphill 5 % | 3:57 | Downhill 5 % | 2:56 (fastest) |
| Uphill 10 % | 4:31 | Downhill 10 % | 3:30 |
| Uphill 15 % | 5:17 | Downhill 20 % | 4:58 |
| Uphill 20 % | 6:17 | Downhill 30 % | 7:03 |
| Uphill 30 % | 7:54 | | |
| Uphill 40 % | 9:57 | | |

Running along a slope costs nothing extra. Holding the walk key caps your speed at a third of Full Speed. Reading the map while running caps it at 70 %.

### Read the map while running

1. Press M or Tab **(configurable)** to bring the map up or put it away, or hold Space **(configurable)** to hold it up only while the key is down.
2. Turn the map with Q and R **(configurable)**, or press F **(configurable)** to align it to the current Leg (not on Score, which has no Legs), or N **(configurable)** for north up.
3. Zoom with the mouse wheel, or keypad + and − **(configurable)**. Drag the map with the right button.
4. Hold C **(configurable)** to look at the compass alone, without the map.

### Look at a Run again

1. In the main menu, press **Analyse**.
2. Under **Order** at the top right, choose **Newest first** or **Best time first** for the Runs of each Course Version. The list keeps your choice.
3. To see only the Runs of one Orienteering Type, choose it under **Orienteering Type**, left of Order: Events and Courses without a Run of that Type are left out. It lists only the Types you have run, and starts on **All Types** each time.
4. Double-click a Run in the list to open its analysis. To delete a Run, select it and press the bin (🗑) below the list, or the Delete key, and answer **Delete**: it moves to the system's trash and cannot be analysed again. Analyse opens with the choices you left it with last time: Show, the boxes, the legend, the replay's speed and Tail, Text or 3D, Runner's view and Trees.

### Compare Runs

1. In the analysis, look at the **Legs table** below the map. Its rows are every Run of the Course Version, yours and other Profiles', its columns the Legs and the Total.
2. Above it, under **Show**, choose **Split Times**, **Time Loss** (behind the fastest Run shown on each Leg) or **Loss to Perfect** (behind the Perfect Race). Untick **Perfect Race** to hide its row, or tick **Only First Sight Runs** or **Only my Runs** to see fewer Runs.
3. Select a Run's name to analyse that Run instead, or one of its times to analyse that Run on that Leg. Select a Leg's title to see that Leg of the selected Run.

On a **Score** Course every Run has Legs of its own, in the order it punched: the columns are the selected Run's Legs, and another Run has a time only on a Leg it ran too (the same two places, either way round). Selecting another Run turns the columns to its Legs. The Runs are ranked by points, then by time, their points before their time. The Perfect Race row has each of the selected Run's Legs at its fastest, and as its Total the time of the Perfect Race's own order, the one that scores most within the time limit.
4. Tick Runs to see their tracks on the map, each in its own colour, which it keeps whatever you select.

### Replay Runs

1. In the analysis, tick the Runs to replay in the Legs table (none ticked: the selected Run alone). To replay one Leg, select it; the Runs then start the Leg together.
2. Press **Play** (the triangle) in the bar above the Legs table, or drag the time slider. Choose how fast it runs (10x at first; 1x to 4x match the game speeds) beside it, and the **Tail**, how much track is drawn behind each runner, at the right.
3. On the slider, a line with a golden star on top is where the fastest of the Runs finished, a line with a pointer at the bottom the selected Run, and blue dashes the Perfect Race, while the Legs table shows it. Drag the slider to its end to see the whole analysis again.
4. To watch it in 3D as well, press **3D** under the legend beside the map. The 3D view takes the place of the text beside the map, the tracks drawn on the ground, and its camera follows the selected runner. Right-drag to turn and tilt it, drag to move, and use the wheel to go farther or closer; the other keys of the editors' 3D View work after a click in it. Press **Text** to get the text back.
5. Untick **Trees** below the 3D view to see the ground without them. To see what the selected Run's runner saw, tick **Runner's view** beside it: First- or Third-Person View as run, the head's turns, the map and the compass when they were up, and the next Control's description, the 3D view shaped as the game's screen. The lines on the ground are left off, and dragging no longer moves the camera. Runs from before 0.8.0 look straight ahead (down at the map while it is up), the map not moved in the hand.

### Terrain Area Editor

- **The map**: the OCAD map, or with **3D View** the built terrain. In the 2D View, the wheel and the + and − keys (keypad too) zoom and dragging moves the map; the map always fills the view. P goes to a map position, L copies what the mouse window says, and Backspace removes a position's mark. A small window beside the mouse gives the map position, and the height and Runnability when those layers are shown.
- **The panel**: the house (⌂, Back to menu) on a row of its own, opening (**OCAD File…**, **Terrain Area…**, **Generate Random Map…**), **Symbol Links…** with the state of the links, the **Terrain Area Boundary** buttons, **Terrain Style** with **Terrain Styles…**, **Contour Interval** with **Build Terrain Area** and **Slopes…**, the **Layers** to show (Map, Hillshade, Contours, Slope Direction, Contour Model, Runnability, Props), and **Save…** and **Save As…**.
- **For a random map**, the panel has in place of the Symbol Links, the boundary, the Contour Interval and Slopes… a line saying what the map was made with, and the **Seed** with **Generate new**, and **Copy code** and **Paste code**.
- **Generate Random Map window**: Map Recipe, Size, Scale, Contour Interval, Steepness, Level ground, Settlements, Water bodies, Power lines, Dense vegetation, Dry hilltops, Footpaths, Stone walls, Open forest areas and Seed with New seed; **Reset to defaults** (all but the seed; your settings are kept for next time); **Generate** and **Cancel**.
- **The map's context menu**: right-click the 2D map, when not drawing the Terrain Area Boundary, for **Show full map**, **Zoom in** and **Zoom out** (as the + and − keys do), and under a separator **Export to PDF** (see Export a map to PDF) and **Export Course** (see Export Courses as IOF XML).
- **The 3D View**: the ground with the map draped over it, with boxes for 2D Map, Hillshade, Trees (off at first), Props and Buildings. Tab switches between orbiting a point and flying freely, after a click on the view. A box at its top left lists its keys, which are fixed.

### Symbol Links window

The OCAD File's Symbols with Objects in the map, each linked to a Standard Symbol of the Mapping Standard, skipped, hidden or unresolved. Beside each are its guess and Match (solid, by appearance, by number, mismatch, conflict). A preview of the map shows the selected Symbol's Objects, and can show only the linked, unlinked, Skipped or mismatched Symbols. **Done** returns to the editor.

### Slope Direction editor

Every contour and form line with downhill arrows. Red lines have issues, green ones you changed, dark violet ones from Saved Slope Directions. **Saved Slopes…**, **Check again**, **Build** and **Cancel**, or **Done** when opened from Slopes….

### Saved Slopes window

The Saved Slope Directions by OCAD File: its name, how many contour pieces and when saved last, the latest first, each with the bin (🗑) to delete them after a question. **Close** returns to the Slope Direction editor.

### Terrain Styles window

The styles (Default is built in and cannot be changed), with **New…**, **Rename…** and the bin (🗑) to delete, and the selected style's **Runnability**, **Roughness**, **Terrain Rules** and **Props** by Standard Symbol. Only Runnability and Roughness can be changed so far.

### Course Settings window

Three tabs, **Course**, **Map** and **Run**, and a fourth named after the chosen Orienteering Type with its own settings (left out for a Type without any: Normal, Mirrored, Compass only). **Course**: the chosen Course's name, which no other Course of the Event may have; its **Orienteering Type**: **Normal**, **Score**, **Corridor**, **Window**, **Memory**, **Line**, **Mirrored**, **Compass only** or **Place the flags**; its **Difficulty** in the Swedish colour system, Green to Black, a new Course's your Profile's Default difficulty (Orange at first); the **Course setter**, your Profile's name for a new Course; a **Course description**; **Night orienteering**, kept with the Course but not yet used in Runs (they are in daylight until Night Orienteering comes). **Run**: **Guided Punching** (a Punch at a wrong Control gets no response and is not recorded), **Allow other Profiles to play the Course** (on for a new Course; off, only the Profile that set the Course sees it in the list of Courses on the Run screen, and a Course from before Profiles can always be run), and the **Allowed Aids**: the compass, Show Position and Heading Alignment; a new Course allows only the compass. An aid not ticked is not there in a Run of the Course: no compass, no Show Position in the Run menu, no Heading Alignment key. The **Flag Size** of the Course's flags in a Run, **Small**, **Normal** (a new Course's), **Large**, **Huge** or **No flag**, with a preview of the flag on its whole stand against the forest's colour to its right (the stand lighter and thicker there than in a Run, to be seen); the stand is as tall at every size, a larger flag reaching further down and wider. **No flag** puts no Control flags in the terrain (the Start and Finish keep theirs): a Control is punched within 5 m of where its flag would stand, the circle on the map being the only guide (the Course's tooltip on the Run screen and the Start Countdown say so); the preview is the forest's colour alone, saying "No flag in the terrain". **Map**: the **Map Type** the Course is run and analysed on: the normal map, contours only, contours and cliffs, or no footpaths (the normal map without its footpaths and small paths, 505-508, the roads and all else kept; a map without footpaths shows the normal map), with a preview of a piece of map to its right, with a sample Control on a knoll, pit or small depression near the Course's first Control; it shows the Control's code with its number when **Show control codes** is ticked. A Terrain Area saved before Map Types shows the normal map instead until it is saved again in the Terrain Area Editor; the window says so. **Show control codes** (off for a new Course) prints each Control's code with its number on the map, as 1-31. **Black overprint** (on for a new Course) prints the map's black over the Course on the 2D maps, as on a printed map, so cliffs, boulders and other black details show through the purple; the preview shows it. A Terrain Area saved before Black overprint draws the Course over the map until it is saved again in the Terrain Area Editor; the window says so. **Show Full Course** (on for a new Course) shows the whole Course in full colour; untick it for **Highlight the next n legs** (1 to 9, 4 at first), greyed out while Show Full Course is on: the Run's map shows only the Leg being run and the next ones, n in all, and their Controls in full colour, and the rest of the Course toned down; it moves on as you punch. **Score** (Type): on the Score tab, the **Time limit** (30 min for a new Score Course, 5 to 180) and what **Each minute over costs** (5 points at first), shown only for Score; on the Map tab, Show Full Course and Highlight are left out, as Score has no Legs. Making a Course Score keeps each of its Controls once, listed by code, and finishes it at a Finish of the Event within 50 m of its Start, when there is one. **Window** (Type): on the Window tab, the **Radius** (75 m for a new Window Course, 20 to 300): the Run's map shows only the ground within it of the Start, each Control and the Finish, blank paper elsewhere with the map's north lines drawn whole across it (as on Compass only, and likewise from lines along its north on a Terrain Area saved before Compass only), with the Course drawn whole. **Compass only** (Type): the Run's map is blank paper but for the Course, the map's north lines (drawn whole from border to border, without the gaps the map may have in them) and its border, the edge of the drawn map within the Terrain Area Boundary; each Leg has its length written beside it, the straight distance from place to place to the nearest 10 m (as 320 m), placed clear of the numbers, the circles and the other Legs (also in Preview Course, Analyse unless Full map is ticked, and Map with Course in Export to PDF); the Map Type list is left out, and the Run tab's **Compass** is ticked and cannot be unticked, as the Course is run with it (choosing another Type gives back what it was). The Course is drawn without the cuts you made in its lines and circles (they are kept, should you choose another Type), its numbers where you put them. A Terrain Area saved before Compass only has lines drawn along its north instead of the map's own until it is saved again in the Terrain Area Editor, and one built before has its boundary as the border until it is built again; the window says so. **Mirrored** (Type): the Run is on the map and the terrain mirrored left to right, north still up, so known ground looks new: the map without its text (place names and other words), the Course's numbers and codes reading as usual at their mirrored places, the compass pointing to the mirrored north. Analyse shows the map and the 3D mirrored too, as you ran them; the Event Editor and the window's preview show the map as it is, but with **Preview Course**. A Terrain Area saved before Mirrored shows its map's text mirrored until it is saved again in the Terrain Area Editor; the window says so. **Corridor** (Type): on the Corridor tab, the **Width** of the band along the course line (80 m for a new Corridor Course, 20 to 300, half on each side): the Run's map shows only the ground within it, blank paper elsewhere with the map's north lines drawn whole across it (as on Window), with the Course's places drawn; and **Show the course line** (off for a new Corridor Course), whether the Run's map also draws the lines between them. The editors, Analyse and Export to PDF always draw the line. **Line** (Type): its line is drawn 1.0 mm wide on paper, three times a course line, to stand out against roads and paths, on every map, with the watercourses (304-306) cut out of it, so the blue shows: a gap where it crosses one (a minor water channel's gaps between its dashes count as water too), and where it runs along one a slot inside it, the purple on both sides; a Terrain Area built before Line snapping has no such cuts until it is built again. As the thick line would hide too much of the map, a Line Course always has **Black overprint**: the box is ticked and greyed while Line is chosen, and another Type gives back what it was. On the Line tab, **Rings**, whether the Run's map shows the Controls' rings on the line: **Hidden**, **Shown as found** (a new Line Course's) or **All the time**; and what **A missed Control costs** (5 min at first, 1 to 60); on the Map tab, Show Full Course and Highlight are left out, as the map shows only the line. A Line Course whose line, from flag to flag, crosses impassable ground or leaves the Terrain Area Boundary cannot be saved: the Event is not saved, and the dialog names the Leg and how far along it, and the map marks each place where the line crosses impassable ground or leaves the Boundary in red, with a red ring where it begins, shown while that Course is chosen; bend the line round. The marks stay until you save again: a place the save finds cleared loses its mark. **Memory** (Type): on the Memory tab, **At Controls only** (ticked for a new Memory Course) with **Within** (15 m at first, 5 to 50), how near the last Control's flag, or the Start, the map can be read; and **Map time per leg** (unticked at first; the two below greyed out while it is unticked) with the **Map time** (10 s at first, 1 to 120) and what **Each second more costs** (5 s at first, 1 to 60). At least one must be ticked, or OK is disabled. **Place the flags** (Type): on the Place the flags tab, **Free within** (5 m at first, 0 to 50), how far off a placed flag costs nothing; **Each metre more costs** (2 s at first, 1 to 60), for each metre beyond it begun; **Far off beyond** (100 m at first, 10 to 500) and what a flag placed further off costs instead, **Far off costs** (5 min at first, 1 to 60); **Ask before placing** (off at first), a question before each flag is placed, with the clock stopped and the map and terrain hidden. The Run tab's Flag Size is left out, as the Course has no flags. An Event where a Control of a Place the flags Course has no feature (column D) in its Control Description cannot be saved: the dialog names it. Changing the Type, Guided Punching, the Allowed Aids, the Flag Size, the Map Type, Show Full Course, Highlight, Black overprint, Score's time limit or overtime, Window's radius, Corridor's width or course line shown, Line's Rings or what a missed Control costs, Memory's settings, Place the flags' costs, or a Corridor or Line Course's line, on a Course with Runs warns that saving makes a new Course Version.

**Settings for all Courses**, opened with **All Controls** chosen: the same window and tabs for every Course of the Event at once, without the name, course setter and description. A Type's own settings, such as Score's, Window's, Corridor's or Line's, show only when every Course has that Type; with Types Mixed, the fourth tab is called **Type settings** and says they are set one by one. A list or box where the Courses differ shows **Mixed** and is left as each Course has it unless you change it; what you change is set in every Course. When some Courses have Runs, a change to what makes Runs comparable warns that saving makes new Course Versions of them.

### Save window

In the Event Editor: the Event's name, fixed for **Save…**, the **Event description** (free text about the Event, shown when opening it, changeable with either button), **Other Profiles may open, edit and save it**, and what saving does to Courses with Runs.

In the Terrain Area Editor: the Terrain Area's name, its text about the map: creator, club, mapper(s), map year, permission and notes, and **Other Profiles may open, edit and save it**.

**Other Profiles may open, edit and save it** (on by default) can be changed only by the file's owner, the Profile that saved it first; for others it is greyed. With it off, other Profiles see the file in the Open window but cannot open or delete it; they can still run its Events.

### Event Editor

- **The map** of the Terrain Area, with the Course and, when **Show** is ticked, its Perfect Race.
- **The map** under **All Controls**: every Start, Control and Finish. With a Course chosen: the Course, the Event's other places toned down.
- **The map's context menu**: right-click the 2D map where nothing is to remove for **Show full map**, **Zoom in** and **Zoom out** (as the + and − keys do), and under a separator **Export to PDF** (see Export a map to PDF) and **Export Course** (see Export Courses as IOF XML).
- **Courses**: All Controls, then each Course by its name and, unless Normal, its Type, as Course 3 · Mirrored or Course 2 · Score 30 min. A Mirrored Course is shown here as it really is, mirrored only with Preview Course.
- **2D View** and **3D View** at the bottom left of the map. The 3D View shows the ground with the map and the Course draped over it, every flag with its code, and the Starts and Finishes; for looking only. Its keys are those of the Terrain Area Editor's 3D View, listed at its top left. Boxes for 2D Map, Hillshade, Trees (off at first), Props and Buildings show under **Course Settings…** while it is shown.
- **The left panel**: the house (⌂, Back to menu) on a row of its own, **New Event…**, **Open…**, **Save…** and **Save As…**, with the Terrain Area's text about the map; **Courses** (All Controls and the Courses, ten shown, scrolling for more; **New Course**, **Duplicate**, the bin (🗑) to delete, ▲ ▼; a Course with warnings in orange, the chosen one too), **Import Courses…** and **Add Controls…** (by Difficulty), and **Course Settings…** for the chosen Course (**Settings for all Courses…** with All Controls chosen), and **Preview Course** under it.
- **The right panel**: **Map Tools** at the top, with ↶ and ↷ for Undo and Redo at the right end of its row (Add Start, Add Control and Add Finish on one row; Move, Move Flag, Cut and Delete on the next); the description sheet, without a header (the Course's name is in the list of Courses): for the chosen Course the Start's row (△ in column A, the Start's number in B when the Event has several), a row for each Control with its padlock, ▲ ▼ and ✕ beside it (on Score its points and ✕), and the Finish's instruction row (taped route, navigate to the funnel, or navigate with no tapes; click the row to choose); a Start or Finish not placed is a grey row saying so. Under All Controls the Event's Starts, every Control by code with its padlock and ✕ (deleting it from the Event, asking first when a Course uses it), and its Finishes. Where column A has no number, under All Controls and on Score, the Controls' rows are lettered there A to Z, then AA, AB…, in half-tone black, to find them by; the letters are not part of the Control Descriptions. Ten rows shown, scrolling for more; the selected row has a pale blue band, and a place with warnings on the map has its code and frame in red. The sheet stays white paper in the dark theme too. Click a column, C to H, to choose its symbol; A or B to select the row. Under the sheet **Suggest from map** and **Locked**, for the selected row; a Start or Control selected on the map that is not on the Course's sheet, such as another Start, shows its row alone above them. Then **Points by code tens** on Score, **Straighten Leg** on Corridor and Line, a line with the Course's length (along the course line on Corridor and Line; on Score its time limit, Controls and points instead) and, once computed, the Perfect Race's length, climb and time, and **Compute Perfect Race**; the warnings, under a Course only about that Course (its places, name and Perfect Race), under All Controls the first ten.
- **No status line.** When an Event or its Terrain Area cannot be opened, or the Event cannot be saved, a dialog says why; after saving, a dialog lists the Courses that became new Course Versions, and nothing is shown when none did. Something refused from a button beside the sheet's rows, such as ▲ ▼ making a Control come twice in a row, is said in a box at the map's right edge, beside the panel, until you click again (in a dialog while the 3D View is shown).
- **The panel buttons** in the top left corner of the map, one under the other, over the 2D and the 3D View alike (the 3D View's keys listed below them), as in VS Code's activity bar: two buttons, **Event and Courses** (the list with a Control's circle; **Ctrl+B**) for the left panel and **Control editing** (the grid; **Ctrl+Alt+B**) for the right one. Each collapses its panel, and shows it again, giving the map the room; the open panel's button has a green bar beside it. The panels are always both open when the Event Editor opens. A collapsed panel never opens by itself: its button gets a red dot while it holds a fault (a Course in red for Event and Courses; a red row or a warning for Control editing). What is in a collapsed panel, such as Save… or the house, is reached by showing it again; Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y work with the right panel collapsed.
